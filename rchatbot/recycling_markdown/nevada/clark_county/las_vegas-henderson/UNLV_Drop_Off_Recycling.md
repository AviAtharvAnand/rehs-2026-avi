# UNLV Drive Up, Drop Off Recycling

Source: UNLV Facilities Management - Rebel Recycling - Drop Off Recycling  
Source page shown in document: https://www.unlv.edu/facilities/drop-off

## Drive Up, Drop Off

The Drive Up, Drop Off (DUDO) program started in October 1998 to give faculty, staff, and students living in apartments or condos an opportunity to recycle because multi-family housing units do not provide recycling to tenants.

The dumpsters were provided through a grant from the Nevada Division of Environmental Protection (NDEP).

The community is encouraged to use the bins.

## Hours

The DUDO is open:

- 7 days a week
- 24 hours a day
- 365 days a year

## Accepted Items

- All paper, including cereal boxes and paperboard
- Books
- Cardboard
- Plastic bottles, containers, jugs, or tubs marked #1, #2, or #5
- Aluminum
- Steel
- Glass

## Items Not Accepted

- Food or items with food residue, including greasy pizza boxes
- Wet items, liquids, or wet waste that could cause mold or contamination
- Plastic bags
- Tissues
- Paper towels
- Napkins
- Disposable or fountain cups
- Disposable plates
- Disposable utensils
- Styrofoam cups
- Styrofoam packaging
- Styrofoam peanuts
- Manila envelopes with plastic bubbles
- Tyvek or overnight envelopes
- Candy wrappers
- Potato chip wrappers
- Food packaging
- Labels or sticker paper
- Aerosol spray cans
- Photographs
- Transparencies
- Carbon paper
- Wood
- Batteries
- Hazardous materials such as motor oil or gasoline
- TVs
- Computers
- Monitors
- Mirrors
- Plate glass
- Chinaware
- Ceramics

For TVs, computers, and monitors, the source suggests trying the Blind Center of Nevada, Best Buy, or Office Depot.

## Sorting Instructions

Everything can be commingled together except glass.

The recyclable materials are sorted at the same facility as recyclable materials collected through the Valley's curbside program, at the Material Recovery Facility (MRF) in North Las Vegas.

Glass is kept separate because it can contaminate paper.

### Commingled Dumpster

All accepted materials except glass can go into the commingled dumpsters.

- Materials should preferably be loose
- A paper bag may be used
- Avoid using plastic bags
- There are typically three or four commingled dumpsters available

### Glass Bottle Dumpster

- Rinse bottles
- Remove caps
- Put removed caps with other recyclable materials in the commingled dumpster
- Do not break glass
- Keep all glass jars and bottles separate from other recyclable materials
- Do not include mirrors, plate glass, chinaware, or ceramics

### Cardboard Shed

- Corrugated cardboard only
- Cereal boxes and similar paperboard materials should go in the commingled dumpster
- Flatten cardboard to conserve space
- Small cardboard may be placed in the commingled dumpster

## Cleanliness and Contamination

- Keep the DUDO area clean
- Bags left next to dumpsters should be securely tied
- Boxes of paper should be weighed down to avoid wind-blown litter
- Bring clean containers, especially milk jugs, steel cans, dog or cat food cans, and detergent bottles
